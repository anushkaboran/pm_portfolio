In Progress · CalHacks
Rebuild
Rebuild helps people after a flood figure out what they lost and prove it. It does the first pass of the inventory, then lets you and others check it together.
StatusBuilding now
EventCalHacks
ScopeOne complete path for one room
The Problem
After a flood, people sit down with a pile of phone photos of a wrecked room and a mess of old receipts, and try to build a list for insurance or aid applications. It is slow, stressful, and easy to get wrong at the moment people can least afford mistakes.
The Idea
Rebuild pulls scattered evidence together, points out what needs clarifying, lets people sort it out together, and ends with a record where every item can be traced back to its evidence. The demo is meant to prove that one idea.
The Flow We Are Building
✓
1. Create a case: for example, "Living room flooded."
✓
2. Upload evidence: photos of the room and any receipts.
✓
3. Run the agent: it lists the belongings it can see, describes the visible damage and where in the photo to look, reads the receipts, and links a receipt to an item only when there is a specific detail like a brand or model. When something is unclear it flags it or asks, like "Is this the same chair in both photos?" or "When did you buy this?"
✓
4. Review together: people check the findings, answer the questions and fix mistakes. Their corrections are stored separately from the AI's guesses, and later runs never overwrite something a person confirmed.
✓
5. Export a recovery record: an inventory where every item points back to the photo or receipt that supports it.
Key Product Decisions
✓
People stay in charge: human corrections are saved separately from AI guesses, and a confirmed answer is never overwritten by a later run. The AI proposes, people decide.
✓
Only link what the evidence supports: a receipt attaches to an item only with a specific detail like a brand or model, and unclear cases become questions instead of guesses.
✓
Everything traces back: every inventory item points to the photo or receipt behind it, so the record is something a person can defend.
✓
Deliberately out of scope: shopping for replacements, analyzing insurance coverage and submitting claims automatically were left out so one path works end to end.
How The Pieces Fit
Each piece has a different owner: a React frontend that shows the evidence and captures review actions, a FastAPI backend that handles uploads, calls the agent, validates the result and runs the export, the CREAO agent that investigates and reconciles the evidence, and SpacetimeDB, which stores the shared case so everyone sees changes live.

CollabMatch
"The right creators. The right audience. The right budget." A two-sided web app that matches small local businesses with local micro-creators and recommends the best team of creators that fits the business's budget.
RoleProduct, frontend and pitch lead
Duration24 hours · DubHacks 2026
Team2 students
ModelFree for creators, $0 / $29 for businesses
Visit the Live Site → View Repo on GitHub →
The Problem
Small businesses want to work with local creators but do not know who to pick, what a fair price is, or how to split a small budget. Creators with small but relevant local audiences struggle to get any brand deals. Follower count does not tell a café whether a creator's audience is its customer, and manual outreach takes hours.
Who It Is For
Small businesses (the paying customer): independent cafés, restaurants, boutiques, yoga studios and beauty shops with roughly $100 to $500 to spend and no marketing team. Micro-creators (free): creators with roughly 1.5K to 40K followers, often students, posting about food, local spots, fitness, fashion or beauty. Starting market: Seattle cafés and food spots around the U District and Capitol Hill, then expand neighborhood by neighborhood and category by category.
What It Does
The whole product is one loop. A café owner types one sentence, like "$300 to promote my new matcha drink to college students in Seattle." AI turns it into a campaign brief, a transparent scoring engine ranks creators with reasons, an optimizer picks the best combination of creators within budget, and the business invites them in one tap with AI-written messages. Creators join with "Continue with Instagram", get a verified profile built from their real posts, and accept, decline or counter the fee. Both sides track the collaboration to completion.

Key Product Decisions
✓
The flagship is a budget optimizer, not a directory: the rule for the build was not to make an influencer directory with a chatbot attached. The value is the working pipeline: campaign, explainable matching, budget-optimized team, invitation, acceptance.
✓

✓

✓

✓
Businesses can invite creators from social media: a personal invite link solves the cold start, and every invite grows the supply side. No scraping and no automated DMs, because both break platform terms.
✓
A single-creator result is valid: the optimizer is about the best use of the budget, whether that is one creator or four, and the UI shows the leftover budget.

How Fit Is Scored
Every creator gets a 0 to 100 fit score from a weighted formula, and every factor comes with an evidence line the owner can read, like "Reaches college students, young professionals." Creators who cannot work for the campaign are shown under "Not a fit" with a reason, such as fully booked, over budget or outside the city, instead of being hidden.
Audience 25% Content niche 20% Location 15% Budget fit 15% Content style 10% Engagement 10% Platform 5%
The Optimizer, and How We Fixed It
The optimizer checks every affordable team of 1 to 4 creators and picks the one with the most useful fit, while penalizing teams whose audiences overlap. For the demo, a $300 matcha campaign returns three creators who cover three neighborhoods for exactly $300. Iteration: the first version summed raw scores and picked four weak, cheap creators. We changed it so only fit above a floor of 55 counts, and tuned the overlap penalty, which produced the right team. Reach is always labeled an estimate, never a fact.

Business Model and Competition
Phase 1: freemium. Starter is $0 for one active campaign. Business Pro is about $29 a month for unlimited campaigns, the full optimizer, AI proposals and tracking. Phase 2: about 10% of each completed paid collaboration, with escrow. Later: creator add-ons and partnerships with neighborhood business associations. All prices are hypothetical and meant to be tested.
Competitors: enterprise platforms such as Upfluence, Aspire, Grin and CreatorIQ cost roughly $2,000 to $5,000 a month. Collabstr, the closest marketplace, has a free plan with a 10% hiring fee and Pro at $249 a month, but you still pick creators yourself. Small-business tools like Social Cat and Stack Influence aim at e-commerce sellers. Our difference: we build a team, not a search result, every score is explainable, the brief is one sentence, and it is priced for a $300 campaign.
Risks and Scope Calls
We planned fallbacks for every failure point: a keyword parser if the AI is slow, an in-memory store if the database is down, and a sample connected profile if Instagram login is not approved. If we ran out of time, the cut list was the dashboard list, the widest-reach plan, TikTok and counteroffers. The two things we never cut were the optimizer and the accept flow.

Before I Forget
"We don't preserve files. We preserve memories." A memory-preservation platform built around loved ones instead of folders — letting families record a grandparent's voice, translate it, and turn it into a keepsake card before the language and the stories are lost.
RolePM & Product Lead
Duration24 hours · AWSHacks 2026
Team4 students
Result3rd Place, Serverless Solutions

The Inspiration
In immigrant families, loss happens quietly across generations — grandchildren grow up speaking English while grandparents speak Hindi, Punjabi, Korean, Arabic, or Spanish. A voicemail becomes something you can hear but not fully translate. A recipe becomes ingredients without the voice behind it. Before I Forget exists so gratitude doesn't have to wait until after loss, while there's still time to ask, listen, and preserve what made someone feel like home.
What It Does
Instead of a dashboard full of random uploads, users create profiles for loved ones — Nani, Baba, Papá, Halmeoni — and each profile becomes a living archive of stories, photos, recipes, and voice notes. Upload a voice memory and the platform transcribes it in the original language, translates it to English, and turns it into a keepsake card with a summary, cultural context, and gratitude message — keeping both versions instead of replacing one with the other.
